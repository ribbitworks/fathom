# Fathom: Visual Design

**Status:** Agreed with the owner
**Last updated:** 2026-11-11

How Fathom's screens look and behave, so every page is built the same way. The conventions every screen shares, with the piece that draws each, are in [UI Conventions](conventions.md). It applies the components and typeface chosen in [ADR 0004](../adr/0004-ui-components.md). The theme in `apps/web/src/theme.ts` holds the values; this document holds the rules.

## Principles

The owner's principles come first:

- **No instructional text:** If a screen needs explaining, the design is wrong. No hints, help lines, or placeholder sentences telling people what to do. This is for text always on screen: a refusal may say what to do next, and a menu or dialog may state a fact needed at that moment, but never how to use the control, as [UI Conventions](conventions.md#words) sets out. Labels are one or two words, and an optional field may add "(optional)" to its label. Familiar patterns, such as a grip handle for dragging, speak for themselves. Saying what an action will do, as a confirmation does, is not instruction, and neither is reference material, such as what each role lets a user do.
- **No assumptions:** Screens add no purposes, defaults, restrictions, or wording the owner has not asked for. Where something is unclear, it is left out and the owner is asked.
- **Words people say:** Every label, note, and message on screen, the server's refusals included, reads as a person would say it aloud: short, with a subject and a verb, such as "Hiring states needs a choice that means Proposed", never a stack of nouns, such as "the only offered choice meaning Proposed", and everyday verbs, such as "the column that uses this list", never "the column drawing on it".
- **Never the second person:** Nothing on screen, the server's refusals included, says "you" or "your". Say what is so, such as "Nothing can be changed on this date" or "This role can't choose an earlier date". Decided by the owner on 2026-11-08.
- **Keyboard-first data entry:** Wherever people enter or change data, everything can be done from the keyboard, and adding many items in a row never needs the mouse: Tab moves between fields, Enter saves and starts the next item, Esc cancels, and arrow keys move between items. Where many records are entered, such as resources, editing works like a spreadsheet, including pasting rows.
- **The bar:** Top-tier modern products, such as Linear or Notion: calm, dense, and obvious.

The rest follow from them:

- **Quiet and dense:** Fathom is a work tool used every day, so pages favour clear structure and plenty of information over decoration. As few borders, lines, labels, and columns as the task allows; metadata, such as who changed something, stays out of the way.
- **One main action per screen:** Every other action is secondary or in a menu.
- **Buttons look like buttons:** The main action is filled. Every other text button has an outline, as Cancel does. A button that deletes or deactivates is red, filled or outlined; retiring is neither, since a retired record can be restored, so Retire stays grey. A toggle, such as Show retired, is tinted while on, and a button inside an alert, such as Try again, is tinted in the alert's colour. Only icon buttons, such as ⋯ and ×, and links within a sentence, such as Show all in the change history, have no outline, decided by the owner on 2026-11-05.
- **Controls when needed:** Controls that are not always needed, such as grip handles for dragging, appear on hover or keyboard focus. A row's "⋯" menu button is always shown, so its actions are easy to find.
- **No wasted space:** Layout serves the content; nothing takes width or height it does not earn.
- **Colour means something:** Neutrals carry the page. Sea blue marks actions and the current place. Other colours appear only for meaning, such as green for active, yellow for changes worth checking, and red for problems, except for the colours people choose for releases on the [Releases timeline](#components) and for teams on the [Org chart](#components).
- **Never colour alone:** Anything shown by colour is also shown in words, visible or read aloud by screen readers.
- **Both modes:** Fathom follows the computer's setting until a person chooses Light or Dark in their account menu; System follows it again. The choice is kept in that browser alone, and applies on every page, signing in included. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06.
- **International:** Names and text in any language and script are shown, entered, searched, and sorted as well as English is: no screen assumes Latin letters, a given name first, or one name per person, and text is never cut so a character breaks. Search ignores letter case and accents, such as é or ё, but not marks that make another letter in other scripts, such as Japanese が, though Cyrillic letters such as й also match their plain letter. Sorting, dates, and counts follow each person's own language settings, and scripts the typeface lacks fall back to the system's fonts. The [seed data](development-setup.md#fictional-seed-data) includes names in other scripts, so every screen is tried with them.

## Layout

- **Frame:** A deep-water sidebar on the left, the same in both modes, groups pages into sections. The sidebar shows only the pages the user's role allows. A page still to come is shown marked "Later", so people can see what Fathom will offer them; none is now, since Capacity, the last, was started on 2026-11-11, and the mark comes back with the next such page, as [Issues](../issues.md#development) records. Fathom opens on the Overview, or, for a user who may not see the plan, on the first page their role allows, or Help's Roles page if there is none. Help is the last section, open to everyone, with its own icon for each page. A top bar names where the user is and holds their account menu, wide enough for their email address, which it starts with, then the role they act as: its name alone for a user with one role, or, for a user with several, a Role group listing each with a tick beside the one acted as, where choosing another switches to it at once, without signing in again, and the page follows, giving way to the first page the new role allows if it does not allow this one. Change name and Change password follow, then Appearance, with System, Light, and Dark and a tick beside the one chosen, then Sign out.
- **Collapsed sidebar:** The sidebar collapses to a rail of icons, each naming its page on hover or focus, and remembers the choice.
- **Narrow screens:** The sidebar opens from a menu button, and closes when the user moves to another page.
- **Pages:** Each page starts with a heading and the page's main action on the right. Content sits in bordered panels on a canvas a shade darker than them.
- **History pages:** Change history and Access history, in the sidebar's History section, look alike: a search field, like a table's, then one row for each entry, written as [UI Conventions](conventions.md#history) sets out. The change history has a sentence for each record a save changed, such as "changed the list Sites: Name from Sites to Locations"; records of a kind added or deleted together share one, such as "added 150 people", naming ten, with Show all for the rest. A save that changed several records lists five, with Show more for the rest. Show more at the end of the page reads older entries. The change history's search looks on the server in who made each change and the names and values involved, reading back through up to 2,000 saves at a time, with Show more to read on, and shows what matches first in each entry; the access history's searches the latest entries it shows.
- **Roles help:** Help's Roles page names each starting role beside what it lets a user do, with a sea-blue check, read aloud as "Current role", before the user's own role, which comes first when it is a role made later, followed by what each sensitivity covers. Wherever a role is chosen, each role shows its description under its name.
- **About Fathom:** The last item in Help, open to everyone, opens a box in the middle of the screen, over the current page, rather than going to another, unlike dialogs, which open near the top, since it asks nothing of the user, with Fathom's mark and name above three rows: Version, such as 0.3.0, or 0.3.0, plus 4 commits, or Unreleased, as [Decisions Log](../product/decisions.md#platform-and-operations) sets out; Commit, the short commit, followed by "modified" for a build from files that differ from it; and Committed, the commit's date, such as November 2, 2026. A build given nothing from git, as [Development Setup](development-setup.md#ways-to-run-fathom) describes, shows each as Unknown.
- **Drafts help:** Help's Drafts page names each mark a person, list, or choice with a draft can show, drawn as on the People table and the Lists page, beside what it means, followed by what happens to a draft once saved, approved, rejected, or discarded, and what each choice in the Draft menu shows.
- **Roles:** Each row names the role, what it is for, and how many active accounts hold it, and ends with Edit and Copy buttons, or, for a user who may see roles but not edit them, a View button; New role is the page's main action, shown only to those who may edit roles. View opens a read-only dialog, titled with the role's name, with its name, what it is for, and the grid and summary with every box fixed, and Close as its one action. The role dialog sets what a role grants in three parts: a grid of the areas it reaches, with View, Approve and Edit; a grid of the data classes, with View and Edit, General reading With the area and Personal and Confidential each named with its lock mark; and Classify data and Approve own drafts as boxes of their own, each with what it allows under it. Beside them, a summary in plain English says what the role gets, the pages and the views needing classified data, what it doesn't get on the pages it reaches, each with the classes and details it needs, such as "Org chart on future dates: needs Confidential (Future reorganizations)", and what its holders can and cannot do. A tick that comes with another, such as View with Edit, is greyed. As boxes are ticked, a line added to the summary shows in bold teal before settling to plain text, and a line taken away is struck through in red and fades before it goes, each over three seconds; the lines below then close up. A permission that does nothing without another is named in a yellow box. Before a change to a role held by accounts is saved, the dialog lists each account it changes, with what it gains and loses, the user's own marked as theirs, with Back to return to the grid. When what a starting role grants changes, its What it is for text is emptied, unless it was changed by hand, and a dimmed line under the empty field says "What the role grants changed, so its old summary is gone.", to be written again or left empty; putting the grants back brings the text back. A starting role offers Restore template, as an outlined button on the left of the dialog's foot, which puts back Fathom's version for saving.
- **Lists:** Beside the search, a switch shows All lists, those Built in, or Custom ones, kept in the page address like the search. A list Fathom builds in, such as Positions, has a "Built in" label beside its name, in the table and in its panel, and a dimmed line from Fathom under its choices saying what the list is for, starting, for those who may change lists, with "These can be renamed, added to, or retired."; a description the organization gives shows under the name, as for any list. Its dialog has no Retire list or Delete list button. In a [list with meanings](../product/decisions.md#configuration), each choice shows what it means after its description, chosen from a menu when the choice is added or edited. The empty row starts on the first meaning, keeps the one last chosen, and gives it to every pasted line. When a choice is the only one that means something, its menu greys out Retire and Remove, with a line saying why, and its meaning cannot be changed; the last choice offered in Positions has Retire and Remove greyed out the same way.
- **Tier names:** The words Personal and Confidential, where running text names those tiers, such as a role's description, are bold, Personal in orange and Confidential in red, deep in light mode and bright in dark. A class's mark on a column or list is a grey box instead, as a fact is, as [UI Conventions](conventions.md#access) sets out.
- **Accounts:** For a user who may see administration but not edit it, the page has no New account button and no Edit buttons. Each row names the account's roles under Roles, the default first and marked "(default)" when it holds several, and ends with an Edit button, the account's one action, opening a dialog, shown only to those who may edit administration, that changes its name, email address, and roles, chosen from every role by tick boxes under Roles, each with its description under its name, and, once several are ticked, its default role, chosen by radio buttons under Default role naming the roles ticked; unticking the default makes another of those left the default. The dialog has Reset password and Deactivate, or Reactivate, as outlined buttons on the left of its foot, Deactivate in red, each asking to confirm. A new account starts on Team Member. The user's own row has a sea-blue "This account" badge beside their name, lighter in dark mode, and offers no password reset. Under the list of roles, for a user who may edit roles, a dimmed line says "Custom roles are created on the Roles page.", with Roles linking there. New account asks for no password: once the account is created, and once a password is reset, the dialog shows the password Fathom generated as grey monospaced text on a grey ground, with no field's border, hidden until the eye button beside it shows it, as on the sign-in page, and selectable once shown, with Copy beside it, which copies it hidden or not, and Done as its one action, saying Fathom shows it only once; a click outside the dialog does not close it. Controls for changes the server would refuse are turned off: a ticked role whose removal would leave no active account able to manage accounts, or none able to edit roles, and, for such an account, Deactivate.
- **Settings:** One panel for each setting, under the page's heading. For a user who may see settings but not change them, the fields are read only and the panels have no Save. Time zone is a list that narrows as a time zone's name is typed, with the day and time there now under it, by the reader's own clock, such as "It is Thursday, November 5, 2026, 2:32 PM there", which follows the choice before it is saved and keeps to the minute. Save is the panel's one action, turned off until the choice differs from the saved one. The Passwords panel has two parts, each under a small heading: Password requirements, with Minimum length and a Must have list of checkboxes for an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter, a digit, and a symbol, above a dimmed line saying changes apply the next time each person changes their password; and Generated passwords, with Length, a Use list of checkboxes for uppercase letters, lowercase letters, digits, and symbols, naming the symbols, and a checkbox to leave out characters that look alike, naming them. While the recipe would break the rules, a red box says why, one line for each reason, and the panel's Save, its one action, stays off, as it does until something differs from the saved settings.
- **Data classes:** In the sidebar's Administration section, after Settings, for a user whose role may Classify data. Under the page's heading, three headed sections, Built-in details, Columns and Lists, each a table naming each item, its class, as a lock box for Personal and Confidential and in words for General, and what the item is about, such as the pages, views and columns needing a detail; the name and class stay on one line, and the third column wraps. Each row ends with Change class…, opening the dialog that changes its class, turned off with a line saying why for a role that can't see its class now. A column drawing on a list has none, since it takes its list's class. Columns and Lists show only to a role that reads them.
- **Org chart:** In the sidebar's People section, after People. Under the heading, a Find a person or team field on the left, which narrows to the names typed, listing people, each with their position under their name, then teams, each with "Led by" and its manager's name, choosing a team finding its manager with their reports shown, with an × to clear it whenever it holds any text, and which, while one team is shown alone, names those outside the team who have the name typed rather than saying nobody has it, then the Draft menu, choosing what to show as on tables, without its filter or deciding drafts, then, for those who change people, Teams, and Show levels, zoom + and −, and Fit to screen on the right. Show levels opens a menu of 1 level, 2 levels, and so on, then All levels; choosing one fits the chart to the panel, while hiding or showing one manager's reports leaves it where it is. Below them, the chart fills a panel to the foot of the window, drawn top to bottom. A manager's reports who manage nobody are stacked in a column under the manager's left edge, each joined by a line from it, and the managers among their reports are side by side to the right of the column, so a large organization grows down rather than across. Each person is on a card with their name, in semibold, their position, dimmed, and the name of the team they lead, if any, smaller and dimmed, after a small dot of the team's colour when it has one, joined to their manager by a line. For those who edit people, that dot, or a dashed circle on a named team with no colour, opens a menu of the ten colours, each with its swatch, and No colour, with the current one ticked; choosing one redraws the chart at once and offers Undo. For those who change people, the team's name is underlined, as a button opening a field with the hint Team name and an × to empty it, to name, rename, or, left empty, remove the name, saved as a draft, with a notification saying the team's name becomes the new one, or comes off, once approved. Teams opens a panel on the right listing every manager's team, named ones by name, then unnamed ones by their manager's name, each named one with its dot, or the dashed circle, in a square button opening the same menu as the dot on a card, then its name, or Add a name in blue where it has none, and Led by and the manager's name. A card on a team with a colour, or below one whose team has none, except the top card of a team shown alone, is topped with an 8-pixel band of that colour, standing above the card so its text sits as on any other, and the line to it is drawn in the colour too; the colour only repeats what the lines already show, so it needs no words of its own. A manager's card has a small org chart button at the top right, Show only this team, and ends with Hide 3 reports, or, once hidden, Show 3 reports, counting the people who report to the manager directly. With one team shown alone, the heading's right reads "Only the team of", the manager's name, and "is shown", with Show everyone beside it. The chart opens on the top two levels, fitted to the panel, and never enlarged past the cards' own size. Dragging pans it and the wheel zooms. A person found is centred, with a sea-blue ring around their card, which turns its team band sea blue too, and any manager above them hidden is opened. A manager whose end date has passed has a red Left box after their name, as on People, for readers who see Personal details; others see their card without the mark, opening nothing, since People does not list them. Clicking a person, or their name from the keyboard, finds them and shows their details beside the chart, as Person details sets out, and the chart keeps them in the middle of the space left beside the panel as it opens, is resized, or closes, until the reader moves the chart. For others, a person's card opens nothing, and the chart keeps its place in the address by Fathom's own ID for each person. With Drafts beside live, a person drafted to report to someone else is drawn under their new manager, their card tinted as a draft row is, with the draft's dot before their name, and a copy under their current manager, light green, faded, with a dashed edge and a dashed, faded line to it, a green dot before the name, which opens nothing; a drafted position or team name has the draft's dot before it, with the live one, if any, under it, dimmed, after a green dot, on a taller card. A moved card whose draft takes effect from a day other than the one shown says so on a dimmed line of its own under the position, From and the day, such as From Oct 1, 2026, on a taller card. Drafts applied draws the chart as it will be once the drafts are approved, with people added and removed and their details changed, and Live only draws it as approved, read only, without naming or colouring teams. The choice is kept in the address. A planned hire is a placeholder card after the people under its manager, with a grey dashed edge and no shadow, its name, or, with none, its ID in grey italics, so the name fits, then its position, as on a person's card, and a grey Planned, expected box with its month, such as Planned, expected Mar 2027. It opens nothing. A planned manager's card heads its planned hires, with Show only this team, and is not listed in Teams, since a planned team has no name until it is filled.
- **Feature teams:** On the Org chart, a two-part switch at the heading's right, Reporting teams and Feature teams, chooses which teams the chart draws, kept in the address, with the person found or open kept found and centred. On Feature teams, each team is drawn under its feature lead's card, which names the team after a square of its colour, as a manager's card names their team, or, while it has no lead, under a card of its own named after the team, with No feature lead as its position; its members are stacked under it, so someone on two teams has a card under each, and the finder lists each card with its team under the name in place of the position, and a team's own card as a team, with No feature lead. Cards say Hide 3 members, there is no Show only this team, and a lead's card offers no naming or colouring of their reporting team. A Feature teams button, in place of Teams, opens a menu of the teams, each after a square of its colour and marked when it has a draft, then, for those who edit feature teams, New feature team. The square before a team's name is to become the team's name in a tinted box, as [A coloured name](conventions.md#values) sets out. Choosing a team, its name on its lead's card, or a team's own card, opens the team in a side panel beside the chart, laid out as [Details Panels](conventions.md#details-panels) sets out, centring the chart on the team's first card and keeping it centred as the panel slides in. The panel is titled with the team's name, marked when its draft adds or removes the team, then Details: Name and Colour, the colour after its dot in words, or None; then Members: Feature lead and Members, the members by name with the draft's dot before each a draft adds or removes, one it removes struck through, followed in grey by from and the date where that is not the date shown. Each value that can be changed has its pencil: Name and Colour for those who edit feature teams, Colour saving at once with Undo, and Feature lead and Members, which open a picker of people, each with their position and reporting team under their name, matched by the search too, the lead's beginning with None. Each other change is a draft, saved at once, which Cmd+Z, or Ctrl+Z, undoes. Joining, leaving and a change of lead take effect from the date the chart shows; on any date but today, Name and Colour have no pencil and Remove feature team is not offered, since they take no date. Discard draft, Resubmit for a rejected draft, and, for those who edit feature teams, Remove feature team end the panel; a team they take away closes its panel. New feature team opens a panel of the same rows, Name, then Feature lead and Members, with Cancel and Add, which adds the team with them at once. Those who only see feature teams see the same panel without anything to change. Those who see the chart but not people see teams as approved, without drafts. Clicking a person's card opens their details, which show, in place of the Reporting section, their manager alone, then their feature teams.
- **Projects:** In the sidebar's Plan section, after Overview. The page has four tabs under its heading, Projects, Proposals, Customers, and Milestones, each a table edited like People's, with the tab kept in the page's address. A record's details open in a side panel beside the table, from the small button in its Name cell or Show details in its row's menu, except on Milestones, which has no details of its own. A project's panel shows its dates and its milestones, each with its own menu, and Add milestone; a project's and a proposal's panel ends with Scope history, a row for each feature it was given or had removed, newest first: the feature's name as a link to it on Features, then Added or Removed with the day the change was drafted, in grey, and, for a removal from a proposal, Reason: and the reason given. A draft's rows come first, after the draft's dot, and leave with Live only; a row is never struck through or removed, since history is not undone. A drafted removal from a proposal has a Change reason button, outlined, for those who change the plan. A change that removes features from proposals, on either side, first opens a dialog titled Reason for the removal, saying which features are removed from which proposals, such as Offline Sync is removed from Harbour Cranes Upgrade., with a Reason (optional) box, which has an × to empty it, and Cancel and Save; Save with the box empty removes them with no reason, and Cancel changes nothing, first asking Discard the reason?, with Keep writing and Discard, when the reason has been changed and the dialog is left by Cancel, Esc, or its close button, as [Leaving Unsaved Work](conventions.md#leaving-unsaved-work) sets out. Change reason opens the same dialog, titled Change the reason for the removal, holding the reason given. A project's or proposal's panel shows each of its customers as a link that opens that record's panel on its own tab, each of its proposals the same way, one to a line, and each of its features, one to a line, as a link that opens the feature's panel on Features. A project's Proposals and Features cells, and a proposal's Features cell, and every project or proposal named anywhere, follow [Links to Records](conventions.md#links-to-records), so records with similar names are told apart. Show features in a project's and a proposal's row menu opens Features filtered to the features it names. New opens a form in the side panel, headed New customer, New proposal, New project, or New milestone, with a row for each column a new record has, laid out and entered as in the New form Person details sets out, Status starting as the first status offered, and Project starting as the project the table is filtered to. Add names each problem under its row, with focus on the first, and, once added, the panel shows the record, or, for a milestone, closes. Leaving a form with anything in it, by Cancel, its close button, another tab, or another record, asks first, such as Discard the new customer?, with Keep adding and Discard. Nothing can be left while it is being added. Those who change the plan change each value in a panel, Name included, from a pencil at its right or a click on it, as in Person details below, each change a draft, except a project's dates, which come from its milestones; the pencils go, as on Person details, for a record drafted to be removed or held by another's unpublished changes. Those who only see the plan see the same tables and panels, read only, with no New button. The panel follows the Draft menu: with drafts beside live, a value a draft changes has the draft's dot before it, and its live value under it, dimmed, after a green dot; with drafts applied, only the drafted value; with Live only, the record as it is live, with no way to change it.
- **Features:** In the sidebar's Plan section, after Projects. One table edited like People's, under the heading Features, with New feature beside it. Features are grouped by cycle under full-width headings naming the stream and cycle, such as Platform: 27.1, with how many each holds, streams in their order and each stream's cycles by date, then Later for those that target no cycle. A Group by menu after Metrics, whose button names what is in use, such as Group by: Cycle, offers Cycle, Release, Needed by, and None, kept in the view and cleared by Clear all. Grouped by Release, each release heads its group with its stream, its name and the day it ships, such as Platform: Harbor 2, ships 31 Dec 2026, streams in their order and each stream's releases by date, then each stream's cycles in no release, such as Platform: Not released yet, then Later; a release no feature targets has no heading. Grouped by Needed by, each month a feature is needed by heads its group, shown as Mar 2027, earliest first, then No date. Once a feature is added from New feature, the panel shows it. A feature's details open in a side panel beside the table, as a project's do, from the button at the right of its Name cell or Show details in its row's menu. Its columns are Name, Status, Description, Notes, Customers, Projects, Proposals, Needed by, Effort, Release, Target cycle, and Feature team, then any columns added, in the order added: the order a feature's details show them in, grouped under Details, Customers and commitments, Planning, and Columns, as [Details Panels](conventions.md#details-panels) sets out. Release is read only: it names the release the target cycle is in, such as Platform: Harbor 2, or Platform: Not released yet, and is empty in Later; it is filtered, sorted, and broken down in Metrics as other list columns are, with Later as its empty value, and in a feature's details it is a link that opens the release's stream on Releases. Projects and Proposals name the projects that need a feature and the proposals that propose it, as on Projects; in a feature's details each is a link that opens its details in the same side panel, beside Features, where its own features open the same way. Only those who change the plan change these two columns, each change a draft of each project or proposal it adds the feature to or takes it from, and the feature's row is not marked; New feature does not offer them. Those who may change planning configuration add them from New column, beside New feature, and change them from a column's menu, as on People, in a dialog that leaves out planned hires and who may see the values, and offers only General lists; Delete column shows only to those who may also change features. A column only drafted to be added shows on the table, but its cells cannot be changed until it is approved, unlike People's, and New feature does not offer it. The form is laid out and entered as in the New form Person details sets out, and a Note column's row has a button that opens the note's side panel under the form. A row keeps its heading while edited, as [Staying Put](conventions.md#staying-put) sets out. A feature whose status means Planned or In progress and that has no feature team has a warning mark, No team, in its Feature team cell. The Target cycle column offers every stream's cycles in one list. The [Features Board](#components) is to be a second way of showing the page, with more choices in Group by.
- **Org chart date:** A row under the Org chart's heading, on either view, holds, in order, a Date box, back and forward arrows, a timeline of 24 months either side of today with a mark for each month, named each year, and Today. On Reporting teams, for a role that does not see Confidential data, the timeline and arrows stop at today, and a line under the row reads Future dates need Confidential access.; Feature teams reach future dates with Personal details alone, drawing people as they are today. For a role that does not see other dates, a line reads Other dates need Personal access. in place of the row. The arrows and timeline move by month, to the first day of the month, or to today in the current month. The arrows, timeline and Today are to become the shared [strip](conventions.md#timelines). On any other date, kept in the address, a line under the row says what is shown, with the day, then Changes take effect from this date. where feature teams' members and leads, or on Reporting teams people's managers, can be changed then, or Nothing can be changed on this date. The line keeps its place while today is shown, so nothing below it moves. Where nothing can be changed, the chart is read only, with no Draft menu, and shows teams or reporting lines as approved; where feature teams can be changed, it shows their drafts as on today, and where reporting lines can, it shows drafted managers as they would be on that date, without Teams, naming teams, or colouring them. ## Components

- **Tables:** Lists of records are tables in a panel, using the full width, each text cell on one line and cut short when it does not fit. Every table's column headers look alike: small, semibold, and dimmed, on one line of a set height, and its header row stays in view while its rows scroll. A page of a long table, such as People or Accounts, fills the window, and the table scrolls within it. A row's actions are in a menu behind a "⋯" button at its end, always shown, except on Accounts, where an Edit button holds them all. What a table's rows hold sets how tall they are: People's are dense for editing, and Accounts' show each person's initials, name, and email address. Rows show no audit detail, such as who changed a record or when; dates follow [UI Conventions](conventions.md#dates).
- **Side panels:** Every side panel, such as a record's or the Metrics panel, sits beside the content, which narrows to make room and stays usable. Dragging the edge between them, or moving it with the arrow keys once focused, resizes the panel, leaving the content room, and the width is kept for next time, starting at 640 pixels, the same whichever panels are open: one for a table's panels, such as a note and the Metrics panel, and one shared by the record panels of Lists and Releases. Panels open at once, such as a note and the Metrics panel, are stacked in that place, each scrolling on its own; dragging the edge between two, or moving it with the arrow keys once focused, shares the height differently, leaving each at least a fifth, and each kind of panel keeps its share for next time. On a narrow screen only the top panel shows. A panel slides in from the right over the content as it opens, the content narrowing beneath it, and slides back out over the content as it closes, the content widening first, unless the computer asks for less motion; a panel opened or closed while another stays grows into, or shrinks from, its share of the height. Panels open, close and hold focus as [Panels and Focus](conventions.md#panels-and-focus) sets out. In a side panel, a list of labels and their values shows each label beside its value while the panel is wide, and over it once the panel is narrow, following the panel's own width rather than the window's; a record's details follow [Details Panels](conventions.md#details-panels).
- **Text fields:** As [Fields](conventions.md#fields) sets out.
- **Entering data:** Lists of items are edited in place, from the keyboard, as [Entering Data](conventions.md#entering-data) sets out.
- **Editing like a spreadsheet:** Tables where many records are entered, such as People, work like a spreadsheet. The keys, the empty rows, and refused values work as [Entering Data](conventions.md#entering-data) and [Refusals](conventions.md#refusals) set out; Tab goes on to the next row at the end of one. A column whose values follow from another's, such as Team, which follows from Manager, is read only, and pasting into it is ignored. An empty cell Fathom fills in from other values follows [Values](conventions.md#values). Once something is typed in an empty row, it shows the values a new record starts with, such as the first feature status whose meaning is Possible. Tab goes straight on to typing in the next cell, moving onto a checkbox or read-only cell without changing it, and Enter adds the record; on People, a new row's State decides whether it adds a person or a planned hire, as in the New form Person details sets out. A row being filled in has a hollow dot in its slot, whose tip names what is wrong, such as "Someone already has that ID", or a required column still empty, such as "Site needed"; what is wrong also shows in red after its first value. A value that is wrong shows in red in its cell. An added record shows at once, as a draft adding it, and cannot be changed until it is saved. A column drawing on a list is edited by typing to narrow its choices, which drop down below the cell, and picking one; where several can be picked, a check marks each one picked. A web link column shows each link's text, or the host of its address when it has none, such as example.com, underlined as a link that opens in a new tab, with the address shown on hover; the person's menu offers opening each link from the keyboard. Editing a link opens a small form below the cell with the address, checked once it is left, and the text to show; Tab moves between the two, then on to the next cell, and clicking away saves. Pasting into a web link column takes each cell's text as the address. A web link column is searched and filtered by its links' text and addresses, and sorted A to Z by the text shown. A checkbox column, such as Manager?, shows a checkbox in each cell, checked or empty, in the light green Live row under a draft too; one click, Space, or Enter checks or unchecks it, and typing does nothing. Pasting Yes, Y, X, True, or 1 into it checks it, and No, N, False, 0, or nothing leaves it empty. A checkbox column is filtered by Yes and No, which the metrics count too, and sorted checked first or unchecked first. Each column starts as wide as what it can hold, up to about 30 characters: a column drawing on a list fits its longest choice, so a cell holding several is cut short, a date column fits the longest date, a text, web link, or Number column the longest value it held when the page opened, and a Note column 15 characters. A header fits its name, any draft's dot before it, and any live name under it. The first column has room for a draft's mark until its width is set. No width follows what is typed, so editing never moves the table. Anyone can set a column's width, its Live copy's with it, from 64 to 1,600 pixels: by dragging the right edge of its header, where a short blue line shows on hover, by Shift, Alt and the arrow keys on its header, or by Wider and Narrower in its menu, which show those keys and are greyed out once the column can go no further; Reset width in its menu, or double-clicking the edge, lets it fit what it holds again. A column's width is part of the view, kept in the address and saved views. Each column's menu sets its items apart in groups: sorting; filtering and Break down by; freezing and the column's width; then changing and moving the column. On People, any column can be dragged by its header, or moved with Alt and the arrow keys or from its menu, which changes the view, as saved views keep it.   - **Zoom:** All, Year, 6 months, Quarter, Month, Week and Days show the whole timeline, three years, two years, a year, six months, six weeks or two weeks across the view, never less than the view's width, and − and + show half as much again, or a third less, from two weeks to the whole timeline. The zoom is kept for next time, starting at Year.
  - **Axis:** Two rows of dates. Year, 6 months and Quarter name their own period above the next smaller one: years over half years, such as Jan to Jun 27; half years over quarters, such as Q1 27; and quarters, such as Q1 2027, over months. Month and Week name months over weeks, each named by its Monday, with the days as short marks along the bottom wherever they are far enough apart to see, and Days names months over days, such as Mon 9. All, and zooming with − and +, cut it as finely as its names fit. A name too long for its period is shortened, such as to Jan or Q1, or left out.
  - **Scrolling:** The timeline opens with today a sixth of the way across, and Today goes back there. Changing the zoom or the window's width keeps today there, or, once the reader has scrolled, the day in the middle of the view.
  - **Strip:** The [strip](conventions.md#timelines) of quarters sits between the zoom and the timeline, with ‹ before it, and › and Today after it, Today shown while today is on the timeline, decided by the owner on 2026-11-11. Each quarter is as wide as its days, named such as Q1 2027, or Q1, or not at all, as its width allows. A quarter chosen, or reached by ‹ or ›, scrolls its start a sixth of the way across, as Today does, ‹ greyed out at the timeline's start and › at its end.
- **Features Board:** The [Roadmap](../product/decisions.md#planning), a second way of showing the Features page, decided by the owner on 2026-11-11 in place of the board [ADR 0009](../adr/0009-roadmap-and-timeline-views.md) proposed, and not built yet. Table | Board, after the page's heading, switches between the two and is kept in saved views. Both share the page's search, Draft menu, Group by, Metrics, Views and Publish. The [strip](conventions.md#timelines) of cycles sits above the board.
  - **Columns:** One stream's cycles, chosen in a Stream menu, as many as fit the width, then that stream's [Later](../product/decisions.md#planning). Each column's header shows its release's name in its colour's tinted box, then the cycle's name, such as 27.1, and its dates; the current cycle's header is marked as today is. No size totals.
  - **Cards:** The ID and name; the status, a Possible one's name in italics, and the priority; the [Size and Risk marks](conventions.md#values); Needed by; for a staffed feature, On track or Late, with its months; and Not staffed for a feature whose status means Planned or In progress and that has no allocations. A card's height follows its content. A Card menu chooses what cards show, of Status, Priority, Size, Risk, Needed by and Staffed months, kept in saved views as a table's columns are. Cards don't show customers.
  - **Rows:** Group by, on Table and Board alike, offers every column drawing on a list or table, such as Feature team, Status, Priority, Size, Risk, Customers, Projects, Proposals, and columns an organization adds, such as Kind, or Product once built, then Release and Needed by. The Board's columns are cycles, so it doesn't offer Cycle.
  - **Order:** An Order menu orders the cards in each cell by Priority, the first, Needed by, Status, most likely first, or Customers, most first.
  - **Moving:** Dragging a card, or Alt and the arrow keys, moves it to another cycle, or to another row, changing that value as a draft, drawn with the draft marks as [Drafts](conventions.md#drafts) sets out. Moving between rows is refused where a feature can have several values, such as Projects, Customers and Proposals, and where the value follows from another: Release follows from the cycle, and Needed by is a month set in the panel. A feature with allocations, or one Done or Cancelled, doesn't move, and trying says why, such as Has allocations, so its cycle can't change. Change its allocations first. Live only shows the board read only.
  - **Details:** A card opens the feature's details beside the board, as on the table.
- **Capacity:** The [Capacity page](../product/decisions.md#planning), in the sidebar's People section, decided by the owner on 2026-11-11, and not built yet. Demand and Settings are the demand forecast and its settings. People and Work share the search, filters, Group by, Views and the capacity total rows; a bar in Work opens that feature's people in People, and an allocation in People links to its feature in Work.
  - **Work:** The [Plan](../product/decisions.md#planning) on the [shared timeline](conventions.md#timelines): work by month, when each piece happens, whether it is staffed, and whether it makes its date. A capacity row for the whole organization, on all staffed work, comes first, then the groups.
  - **Group by:** Feature team, the first, Project, Priority, Status, Risk and Release. Grouped by feature team, each team ends with its capacity row, of person-months allocated against those available, counted as [Capacity views](../product/decisions.md#planning) sets out, and project work, the activities and general work of each project, sits under its own Project work heading.

  - **Later:** Features in Later are listed under the timeline.

Each project is a heading row with a line from its first milestone to its last and a diamond for each milestone, hollow while still to come. - **Dialogs:** Creating and changing settings, such as a list's name, happens in dialogs, which cannot be closed while a change is being saved.
- **Drafts:** How drafts look, and what can be done with them, on every table that has them.
  - **Marks:** How a record, a part of one, or a value with a draft is marked, and a warning about a value, as [UI Conventions](conventions.md#drafts) sets out.
  - **Rows:** As [Drafts](conventions.md#drafts) sets out for a table edited like a spreadsheet.
  - **Columns:** As [Drafts](conventions.md#drafts) sets out for a column's draft.
  - **Lists:** A list and its choices are marked as [Drafts](conventions.md#drafts) sets out for a record made of parts and for a move. A list or choice drafted to be retired or restored stays where it is until approved.
  - **Menus:** A record's menu starts with its name, then a divider. In a table row's menu, a divider sets apart each group present: the table's own items, such as Show details; Approve and Reject; then Resubmit, Discard, and Remove. Its draft section starts with the draft's mark and any rejection's reason, then one line for each change, naming the field in semibold and setting each value apart, as in code, with who made the change. A list's menu has a short section for its own draft and one for a new order of its choices, each with its mark, who made it, and any reason. A click anywhere in a menu but its items closes it.
  - **Publishing:** As [Publishing](conventions.md#publishing) sets out, with Publish in the top bar beside the user's account. A record someone else is changing is read only, and its menu says Being changed by, with who, and that it can be changed once they publish or discard their changes; Approvers cannot decide it, nor the user's own unpublished draft, whose menu says only they see it until they publish it.
  - **Actions:** Those who may edit discard a draft, or resubmit a rejected one; a new order is discarded with Discard new order. Approvers approve or reject a waiting draft from its menu, when their role approves drafts of its area. A rejected draft offers Approvers nothing to decide until it is resubmitted, and a draft that depends on a rejected draft has Approve disabled, with "Waiting for a rejected draft to be resubmitted" under it. A draft this account changed, when its role does not let it approve its own, has Approve disabled, with "Changed by this account, so another approver must approve it" under it; Reject stays.
  - **Deciding several:** The Draft menu offers Approve N drafts… and Reject N drafts… for the waiting drafts shown, under a Waiting drafts label that, when a search, a filter, or Drafts applied hides some, says how many are shown, such as 9 of 11 waiting drafts shown. The dialog says how many related drafts it includes, and how many drafts it skips because they depend on a rejected draft. When the drafts include any this account changed and may not approve, the dialog is titled Another approver needed, names them, and offers only Close. When they include drafts of an area the user's role does not approve, such as the new column a person's draft uses, the dialog is titled Another approver needed too, says "This role can't approve these drafts:", or reject, names them, and offers only Close. On the Drafts page, choosing such a draft disables Approve, with a line saying how many chosen drafts another approver must approve.
  - **Draft menu:** The Draft button above the table, marked while its choices are not the defaults, filters to records whose drafts are waiting or rejected, or that have no draft, shown in a pill such as "Draft: Waiting", and chooses what to show: Drafts beside live, the default; Drafts applied, everything as it will be once the waiting drafts are approved, without live rows or removed records, and with rejected drafts taken back, so the Rejected filter is unavailable; and Live only, everything as it is now, read only, with the filter unavailable. Both choices are kept in the page address and in saved views.
  - **Drafts page:** Every draft as a sentence with what it changes, with a search. Approvers select waiting drafts there to approve or reject; a rejected draft, one that depends on it, or a draft of an area the user's role does not approve, cannot be selected.
- **Undo, not confirmation:** As [Saving and Undo](conventions.md#saving-and-undo) sets out.
- **Empty and failed states:** An empty table says so in a few words. A table that cannot be loaded says so and offers Try again.
- **Messages:** As [Saving and Undo](conventions.md#saving-and-undo) sets out for failures.
- **Styles:** Mantine's components, theme, and style props, such as a one-off size or margin, come first. Anything more goes in a CSS Module, never in a `style` attribute.

## Shared Building Blocks

One component does each job, in `apps/web/src/`. A new screen uses or extends the one listed rather than making another, and a review rates a second one doing the same job as HIGH, as [Reuse across the codebase](../standards/shared/code-review.md#architecture-and-design) sets out.

- **Tables of records:** `RecordGrid` in `record-grid.tsx`, with each kind of cell, its display and its editor, in `cellKinds` in `cell-kinds.tsx`.
- **Search, views, and the bar above a table:** `TableSearch`, `ViewBar`, and `ViewsMenu` in `table-controls.tsx`.
- **Side panels:** `WithSidePanel` and `SidePanelFrame` in `side-panel.tsx`.
- **Feature team labels:** `TeamLabels` in `team-labels.tsx`, to be widened, or renamed, for every [coloured name](conventions.md#values).
- **One tooltip for a whole table:** `TableTips` in `table-tips.tsx`, showing the tip of whatever carries `tipAttribute` under the pointer or in the cell with keyboard focus, such as a team label's whole name or an estimate's hours.
- **Resizing by an edge:** `ResizeEdge` in `resize-edge.tsx`, for side panels and table columns, with a table's column widths in `useColumnWidths` in `column-widths.ts`.
- **A text field with an × to empty it:** `ClearableInput` in `clearable-input.tsx`.
- **Choosing from a list:** `ChoicePicker` in `choice-picker.tsx`.
- **A group of choices in a menu, the chosen one ticked:** `MenuChoices` in `app-menu.tsx`.
- **A column in units:** a column's `units` in `record-grid.tsx`, resolved into the unit and decimal places the view names by `inViewUnits`, with `useChosenUnits` in `record-editing.tsx` for a form or panel's own choice, and an estimate's cell in `EffortCell` in `effort-cell.tsx`.
- **Entering a day:** `DayInput` in `day-input.tsx`. People's date cells and the Person details panel still have their own fields, as [Issues](../issues.md#development) records.
- **Adding a record in a side panel:** `NewRecordPanel` in `new-record-panel.tsx`, driven by a table's columns, with each page drawing its own fields in it: `NewPersonPanel` in `new-person-panel.tsx` for People and `NewPlanRecordPanel` in `new-plan-record-panel.tsx` for the plan's records.
- **A record's details in a side panel:** `Row` for each value, `RecordLink` for a link to another record, `RecordLinkList` for several, one to a line, and `RecordSection` for a headed group of rows, in `record-details.tsx`.
- **Changing a record's values in a side panel:** `useRecordEditing` in `record-editing.tsx`, with what a person's notes add in `person-editing.tsx`.
- **A record's menu outside a table:** `RecordMenu` in `record-menu.tsx`.
- **Something that could not be loaded:** `LoadProblem` in `load-problem.tsx`, with what remains to move over in [Issues](../issues.md#development).
- **The details button in a Name cell:** `ShowDetailsButton` in `show-details-button.tsx`.
- **Notes:** `NotePanel`, `NoteView`, and `NoteCell` in `note-editor.tsx`.
- **Links:** `LinkEditor`, `LinkCell`, and `LinkMenuItem` in `link-field.tsx`.
- **A draft's menu items and deciding drafts:** `DraftItems` in `list-drafts.tsx`, `DecisionModal` in `decision-dialog.tsx`, and `OtherApproverNote` in `draft-details.tsx`.
- **Draft and status marks:** `DraftedItem`, `DraftedValue`, `RowMarks`, `MarkedItem`, `FactBox` and `LiveLine` in `status-mark.tsx`, under the page's `DraftView`, as [Drafts](conventions.md#drafts) sets out.

- **A dialog's buttons:** `DialogActions` in `dialog-actions.tsx`.
- **A column's filter:** `FilterPopover` in `table-controls.tsx`.
