# Tables, Addresses and Search

Part of [UI Conventions](../ui-conventions.md): tables, their views and groupings, what the address keeps, exporting, and search. Each entry gives the rule, the shared piece that draws it, and an example from the [fictional data](../../design/development-setup.md#fictional-data).

## Tables

- **Scroll bars:** Every table, timeline and board scrolls with one style of scroll bar: slim, in the page's greys, and shown whenever there is more to scroll, so a wide view is seen to have more.
  - **Drawn by:** `scrollBars` in `apps/web/src/pages.module.css`, with its size and colour from the theme's `--fathom-scrollbar-size` and `--fathom-scrollbar-thumb` in `apps/web/src/theme.ts`.
  - **Example:** on Features, narrow the window until the table scrolls sideways.
- **Drafts in a table changed elsewhere:** A table whose records are changed outside it, such as in their details panel, as every tab on Releases is, still offers Discard draft and Resubmit in a row's menu for a draft the user may change. A row that stands for its parts' drafts as well as its own discards or resubmits the record's own draft.
  - **Drawn by:** `RecordGrid`'s `canEditDrafts`, one answer for the table or one for each row, such as a cycle's row carrying its release's draft on Releases' Cycles tab, with a table's `ownDraft`, in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on Releases' Schedules tab, a schedule drafted to be retired.
- **A row's controls:** While a table's row is pointed at, or its Name cell has focus, two buttons show at the right of the Name cell: the details button, a box with its right side marked, as the details open beside the table, on a table whose records have details; and a ⋯ that opens the row's menu, so it's in reach without scrolling to the row's end. One row shows them at a time: the row whose menu is open, from its ⋯ or a right-click, else the row pointed at, else the row whose Name cell has focus. The ⋯ at the row's end stays, always shown. A right-click anywhere on the row opens the same menu beside the pointer, except in a cell being typed in, which keeps the browser's own; Shift+F10 and the menu key open it from the keyboard. A record's menu offers Show details, where it has details. There is no row header at the left: the slot before the first value keeps the draft's dot.
  - **Drawn by:** `RowControls` and `MoreButton` in `apps/web/src/row-controls.tsx`, placed by `RecordGrid` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx` in the column its table names as `nameColumn`, with the record's details from the table's `showDetails`, and the menu beside the pointer by `MenuAtPoint` in `apps/web/src/app-menu.tsx`, which a card's `CardMenu` uses too.
  - **Example:** on People, point at any row, or right-click its Start cell.
- **Moving columns:** Anyone who sees a table can move any of its columns, built-in or added. Column order, hiding, freezing and widths are part of the view, never a draft, so each user has their own and nothing needs approval: kept in the address, saved views and [the last view](#tables), and cleared by Clear all and Clear view. Every view starts from the table's own order. A new table is drawn by the shared table, so its columns move too.
  - **Drawn by:** `RecordGrid` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on Features, select Move left in the Priority column's menu.
- **Hiding columns:** Anyone who sees a table can hide any column, required ones included, except the column naming each record, from Hide column in its menu, with Freeze up to this column and the width items, and show it again from the Views menu. A new table is drawn by the shared table, so its columns hide too.
  - **What stays:** A hidden column keeps its filter, whose pill stays so nothing narrows the rows unseen, its place in the sort and its place in the order. Metrics still offer it. Hiding a frozen column leaves one fewer frozen.
  - **What leaves it out:** An export, and so the question before exporting Personal or Confidential data. The empty row skips it, but still names a required one it needs, such as Site needed; a new record's form shows every column.
  - **Shared views:** Hidden columns reveal no value, so they don't decide who sees a shared view.
  - **Drawn by:** `RecordGrid` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`, with the view's hidden columns kept by `withHidden` in `apps/web/src/table-view.ts` and listed by `ViewsMenu` in `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on People, select Hide column in the Site column's menu, then Site under Hidden in the Views menu.
- **Columns hidden at first:** A table may start a column hidden, such as the Key column. Showing one is kept in the view, as hiding a column is, and saved with it.
  - **Drawn by:** a column's `hiddenAtFirst`, with the view's shown columns kept by `apps/web/src/table-view.ts`, and sent and kept by the server as `shown` in `packages/shared/src/views.ts`.
  - **Example:** on People, Key under Hidden in the Views menu.
- **Ticking rows:** A table that acts on several rows at once has a tick box before each row's slot, and one in the first header cell that ticks every row shown that can be ticked and says aloud how many are ticked. Space on a row's first cell ticks it. A row that can't be ticked has its box greyed out. What can be done with the ticked rows shows in the page's heading, as [Actions on ticked rows](pages-and-panels.md#the-page-heading) sets out. A row the view stops showing, by a search, a filter or a tab, is unticked. Rows that act only together are ticked and unticked together, each tick's tip naming the others, three then a count of the rest, such as Linked to the change to Laptop in Equipment; a row linked to one the view hides can't be ticked, its tip ending not shown in the table.
  - **Drawn by:** `RowTick`, `AllTick`, `tickedOf` and `withTicks` in `apps/web/src/row-ticks.tsx`, given to `RecordGrid` as `ticks` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on the Drafts page's Not published tab, as the Engineering Operations Manager, point at the tick box of Equipment.
- **The Key column:** Every shared table whose records have keys has a Key column, showing each record's whole key, as wide as its longest, hidden at first and placed after the column naming each record. It is sorted and filtered by the key's number. A new row, not yet saved, shows none.
  - **Drawn by:** `keyColumn` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`, placed by `RecordGrid` from the table's `keyOf`.
  - **Example:** on People, choose Key under Hidden in the Views menu.
- **Filtering a column:** Every column's filter that offers its empty values, such as Empty, No date or No number, offers every value that isn't empty under it, in the same style: Not empty, or Any date and Any number to match No date and No number, and on a choice column Not empty after Empty, under the choices in use. Ticking one unticks the other, since both would show every row. Every filter's pill names its column, a colon, then what it filters by, in the filter's own words for empty values, such as Onboarding: contains “plan” or Empty, and Start: from Mar 1, 2024 or No date. Not empty shows every row with a value, whatever else the filter holds, so its pill names it alone, such as Onboarding: Not empty. A checkbox column, never empty, offers none.
  - **Drawn by:** `FilterForm` and `describeFilter` in `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx`, with the match in `apps/web/src/table-view.ts` and the filter's `notEmpty` in `packages/shared/src/views.ts`.
  - **Example:** on People, tick Not empty in the Onboarding column's filter.
- **What the Views menu holds:** Beside the sort, saved views and Clear view, the Views menu may hold a choice of which records the table shows, beyond its filters, such as Show retired: a ticked item, first in the menu, kept in the address and saved with the view. Its words say how many records it adds, such as Show retired (1). A page's modes and switches stay where they are.
  - **Drawn by:** each page's declared choices, a `ViewChoice` from `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx` given as `RecordGrid`'s `viewChoices` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`, shown by `ViewsMenu` as a `MenuToggle` from `apps/web/src/app-menu.tsx`, and named in Save view's Includes by `viewParts`.
  - **Example:** Show retired (1) in the Views menu on Releases, once Patches' retirement is approved.
- **Saved views on every page:** Every page with saved views gets them from one hook, which reads the table's views, saves, renames, updates, shares and deletes them, and draws the Save view and Rename view dialogs, so no page has its own. A view keeps the search, filters, sort, groupings, metrics and column choices, under a name unique, ignoring letter case, among the table's shared views and among each user's personal views on it.
  - **Personal:** Seen and changed by its maker alone, its changes in the change history only to them. A view the maker's role can no longer use is sent by name and what it's missing, never its filters, dimmed under My views with a line saying so, offering Rename… and Delete… alone, neither undone.
  - **Shared:** Sharing shows others only changes made after it; stopping sharing, the maker's alone while they may still share, hides them all again. Only the maker, or someone who may share views on the table, updates, renames or deletes one. A shared view is kept from a reader when it filters, sorts, breaks down or groups by a column they can't see, at either level, or groups by data they can't see.
  - **Pages not opened:** A role that doesn't open a table's page is sent none of its views; they are kept, and come back with the page.
  - **The menu:** While a view is applied, Save as new view… sits beside Update view, and Stop sharing current view makes a shared view personal again. The menu names the view the address started from, marked once the view has changed; with none applied, it is marked once the view differs from the table's default.
  - **One view, two forms:** Features' table and Board share one view, which keeps the Board's schedule and card parts but not which form shows. Each of Projects' tabs keeps views of its own, while a view on Drafts keeps its tab.
  - **The last view:** The view a page opened from the menu shows, as [Kept in the address](#the-address) sets out, is kept on the server for each account and page, so it follows the account to another browser. It holds the tab, the view, the saved view it started from and Features' Table or Board, never the record shown, an open form or the time shown, such as People's date, the Board's first cycle or a month grid's quarter. An address naming a view, as a link does, wins and becomes the last view, and one naming a tab other than the page's first shows the last view only if it was on that tab. Clear view makes the default the last view. A last view isn't a draft and has no history, and reading it leaves out what the role can no longer see, as a saved view does.
  - **Drawn by:** `useSavedViews` in `apps/web/src/saved-views.tsx`, keeping the last view through `useLastView` in `apps/web/src/last-views.ts`, which also gives `lastViewAddress`, the address a page opens on, given to `RecordGrid` as `saved`, or straight to `ViewsMenu` on the Org chart, which has no table, with the views in `ViewsMenu` in `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx` and the naming dialog `ViewDialog` in `apps/web/src/view-dialog.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on Releases, type Patches in the search box and choose Save view… in the Views menu.
- **Group by starts with none:** Every table, and a view that shows the same records another way, such as a board, opens with its rows in one list, unless [the last view](#tables) groups them. The Group by menu lists None first, then the table's groupings, such as Cycle, Release and Needed by on Features, and choosing None leaves nothing in the address. A board keeps a grouping chosen in its table where both offer it. While nothing is grouped, the menu's button reads Group by alone, rather than Group by: None; once a grouping is chosen, it names it, such as Group by: Cycle. While rows are grouped, Clear grouping, set apart at the end of the menu as Clear view is in the Views menu, puts them back in one list, with Then by's grouping too.
  - **Drawn by:** the `groupings` of a table in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`, read by `activeGrouping` in `apps/web/src/table-view.ts` and the Group by menu in `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on Features, choose Feature team in Group by, then switch to Board: the rows are feature teams.
- **The groups' order:** Groups run in their first order, earliest first, A to Z or in their list's order, with the group for none, such as No date, No manager or Later, last, on every table and board. While rows are grouped, the Group by menu offers the two orders after its groupings, under a line, named by what the groups are: Earliest first and Latest first for times, such as cycles and months; A to Z and Z to A for names, such as managers, feature teams and projects; and List order and Reverse list order for a list's choices, such as statuses. The second turns the groups round, with the group for none at the top.
  - **The first grouping alone:** Then by's headings keep their order under each heading.
  - **Starting again:** choosing another grouping, None or Clear grouping goes back to the first order.
  - **Part of the view:** kept in the address and in saved and remembered views, and named in Save view's Includes as Group order. A view without it opens with the first order. The button names the grouping alone, since the headings show the order.
  - **Drawn by:** `byGroupRank` and `rankOfNone` in `apps/web/src/table-view.ts`, from each grouping's `holds`, with the choices drawn by `GroupOrderChoices` in `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx`, used by every table and by `FeatureBoard` in `apps/web/src/feature-board-view.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on Features, choose Needed by in Group by, then Latest first: No date is the first heading.
- **A record under several headings:** Grouped by a column holding several values, a record has a row under each of its values, each its own row for focus and the keyboard. A record with no value shows under the column's words for none.
  - **Its mark:** a dimmed +1, or +2 and on, after its name, its tip naming the other headings, such as Also under P-2001 Crane Control. Grouped by two levels, it names each other place by the groups it differs in, both as the first, a colon and the second, such as Also under Priya Raman: Northmere.
  - **Counts:** each heading counts its own records, while the table's count, Metrics and exports count each record once.
  - **One record:** a change in one row shows in all, the arrows step through every row, and a draft marks each, with its Live row under each.
  - **Focus:** closing a record's details puts focus on its cell in the row last used, or else its first.
  - **Drawn by:** `RecordGrid`, with `AlsoUnder` and `focusRecordCell`, in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`; a grouping's `of` gives a record's groups, and `byChoice` in `apps/web/src/choice-grouping.ts` a group for each choice; the Features Board's cards use the same `AlsoUnder`, and `focusCard` in `apps/web/src/feature-board-view.tsx`.
  - **Example:** on Features, choose Projects in Group by: a feature two projects need shows under both, each row marked +1. On People, choose Feature teams: Lorenza Thiel shows under Payments core and Search.
- **Grouping under each heading:** Once rows are grouped, Then by groups them again under each heading.
  - **The menu:** Then by sits under its own label after Group by's choices in the same menu, offering the same columns less the first, starting with None. The menu's button names both, such as Group by: Manager, then Site, so the second level takes no room of its own in the bar. Choosing None, or the second level's column, in Group by takes the second level away.
  - **Its headings:** set in under their first-level heading, smaller and lighter, each counting its own rows. A second-level group a heading has no rows in isn't shown. Headings don't fold.
  - **Not offered:** a page that draws its rows another way, such as a board, or whose rows are made for their grouping, such as the month grids, offers no Then by.
  - **Drawn by:** `GroupingMenu` in `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx`, the view's `thenBy` read by `activeThenBy` in `apps/web/src/table-view.ts`, and the headings by `RecordGrid` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`, with a table's `oneLevel` for rows made for their grouping.
  - **Example:** on People, choose Manager in Group by, then open the menu again and choose Site under Then by.

## Staying Put

- **Nothing moves while it's being changed:** A row keeps its place and its group while it is edited, until the view next changes, so a sorted or filtered table doesn't jump. Records the user adds stay at the end until then, matching or not; those others add appear at the end only if they match. No column's width follows what is typed, and a line that comes and goes keeps its space while empty.
  - **Drawn by:** `RecordGrid` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`, and `apps/web/src/date-control.tsx` for the line under the date row.
  - **Example:** on Features, change Report Builder's Target cycle: it stays under its heading until the page is reloaded.

## The Address

- **Kept in the address:** Everything a page shows is kept in its address: the tab, the mode, the view (its search, filters, sort, groupings, metrics, frozen columns, column order, hidden columns, widths and units), the record shown in a panel, an open form, such as New or Fill, a note's panel, and the date shown. So reloading shows the same, a link shared shows it to others who may see it, and the browser's Back goes back through what was shown. A dialog isn't kept, and neither is a menu: a link's request to open a role's dialog or a column's menu is taken out of the address once used. A page opened from the menu, with no view in its address, opens on the tab, mode and view it last showed the account, but not on a record or a date. Changing the date shown adds to Back's history, as moving to another page does, and so does any other choice of what to show, such as a switch or Show retired; typing in a search replaces it. A chart's zoom and pan aren't kept, and neither is a selection of rows, such as drafts chosen to approve.
  - **Drawn by:** each page's search parameters in `apps/web/src/router.tsx`, with a table's view in `apps/web/src/table-view.ts`.
  - **Example:** on Projects, select the Customers tab and reload.
- **A record's link:** A link to a record is its kind and number after `/go/`, such as `/go/feature/123`, and its key typed after `/go/`, such as `/go/FEATURE-123`, opens the same. The link gives way to the address of the page that shows the record, with its details open, replacing itself, so Back returns to where the reader came from. A filled planned hire's link opens the person who filled it. A link that opens no record shows "Nothing found for" its key, or, to those whose History shows the removal, that it was removed. Following a link records nothing in the access history; the page opened records what it shows, as it always does.
  - **Drawn by:** `recordLinkPath` in `packages/shared/src/record-keys.ts`, `followLink` in `apps/web/src/record-links.ts`, `RecordNotShown` in `apps/web/src/go-page.tsx`, and the server's lookup in `apps/server/src/routes/go.ts`.
  - **Example:** copy Offline Sync's link from its key on Features, then paste it into the address bar.
- **The Org chart's address:** The Org chart keeps feature teams as `teams=feature`, the levels shown as `levels`, a count from the top or `all`, and the team shown alone as `team`, by the manager's Fathom ID. `view=feature-teams` opens Feature teams too, and a team named by its manager's Employee ID opens that team.
  - **Drawn by:** the Org chart's search parameters in `apps/web/src/router.tsx`, with its view in `packages/shared/src/view-tables.ts`.
  - **Example:** on the Org chart, choose Feature teams and 3 levels, and reload.
- **Back to another page:** The browser's Back is the way back. A Back to button is offered only where Fathom itself jumps to another page.
  - **Drawn by:** no page offers one.
  - **Example:** none.
- **Kept in this browser:** A person's own settings for how Fathom looks are kept in the browser, not the address: Light or Dark, the collapsed sidebar, side panel widths and shares, the timeline's zoom, and, remembered by the server for each browser, the role last acted as.
  - **Drawn by:** the piece that owns each, such as `apps/web/src/side-panel.tsx` for panel widths.
  - **Example:** on the Releases page, choose Quarter on the timeline and reload.

## Exporting

- **Exporting what a table shows:** A page whose table can be exported has an Export icon in its heading, before its main action, opening CSV file and TSV file, so the bar above the table keeps its room for filters. A table offers it only once it says what its export holds. The file matches the screen, so what was seen is what is saved: the search, filters and sort, and the columns shown, in their order and units, only those the role sees. An export holding Personal or Confidential data asks first, naming each class in its colour and its columns, as [Data Classes](../../design/pages/administration.md#data-classes) sets out, and is recorded once confirmed and a place to save it chosen.
  - **Drafts:** As the Draft menu shows them: beside live, each drafted record's draft row, then its Live row; applied, the records as drafted; live only, the records as they are. A Draft column says Yes on each row that differs from live, under every choice, and is empty on every other row, so a spreadsheet's filter leaves out either. A Change column says what the draft does, such as Added, Changed, not published, or Added, rejected, and, live only, what a waiting draft would change, as its green dot's tip says.
  - **Rows:** Always flat, whatever the grouping, since spreadsheets filter and sort only rows without headings. Total rows are left out; a record under several headings is in the file once.
  - **Values:** Numbers without their unit, which the heading names, such as Effort (PD); days as 2027-03-31; several values joined by a semicolon and a space; notes as plain text in one cell; checkboxes as Yes or No; a link as its address, or its text and its address in brackets. A value other than a number starting with =, +, -, @, a tab or a carriage return gets an apostrophe before it, so a spreadsheet shows it rather than running it.
  - **The file:** UTF-8 with a byte order mark, so spreadsheets read names in any script, and lines ending in a carriage return and line feed. A value holding the separator, a quote or a line break is quoted, as RFC 4180 sets out, in TSV as in CSV.
  - **The name:** Lower case, its parts joined by underscores and each part's words by hyphens: fathom, the page, the saved view if one is applied, the exporter's account name, drafts-and-live or drafts if it holds any, and the day shown, such as fathom_people_engineers_engineering-director_drafts-and-live_2027-03-31.csv.
  - **Saving:** The browser's Save dialog opens with the name filled in where the browser offers one, as Chromium browsers do; Safari and Firefox follow their download settings. Cancelling saves and records nothing.
  - **Drawn by:** `PageExportMenu` and `useOfferedExport` in `apps/web/src/page-export.tsx`, offered for a table by `useTableExport` in `apps/web/src/table-export.tsx` from the table's `exported` in `apps/web/src/record-grid.tsx`, `ExportQuestion` in `apps/web/src/export-question.tsx`, and the file from `apps/web/src/exports.ts`.
  - **Example:** on People, as the Engineering Director, choose the Export icon, then CSV file.

## Search

- **Finding rows:** A search field above a table finds rows with every word typed anywhere in them, ignoring letter case and accents. Cmd+F on a Mac, or Ctrl+F elsewhere, goes to it from anywhere but a dialog, and Esc clears it, then leaves it, going back to the table where it can. The Key column is left out, since its numbers would match inside other words; a whole key typed finds its record. When nothing matches, the table says so. The Org chart's finder follows the same keys: Cmd+F or Ctrl+F goes to it, and Esc clears it. Typing in any drop-down list, such as the Org chart's finder, finds options the same way.
  - **Drawn by:** `TableSearch` in `apps/web/src/table-controls.tsx`; in a drop-down list, the `byWords` filter every `Select` takes from `apps/web/src/theme.ts`, which any other searchable Mantine list, such as `MultiSelect` or `Autocomplete`, needs too.
  - **Example:** type offline in the search field on Features.
